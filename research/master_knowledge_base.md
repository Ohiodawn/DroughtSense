# Global Agricultural & Drought Knowledge Base

*Consolidated research data from FAO, CGIAR, and World Bank for DroughtSense AI agent context.*

---

## I. Scientific Drought Indices
### 1. Standardized Precipitation Index (SPI)
- **Calculation:** Based only on precipitation.
- **Thresholds:**
    - 0 to -0.99: Mild Drought
    - -1.0 to -1.49: Moderate Drought
    - -1.5 to -1.99: Severe Drought
    - <= -2.0: Extreme Drought

### 2. Standardized Precipitation Evapotranspiration Index (SPEI)
- **Advantage:** Includes Temperature (T) and Potential Evapotranspiration (PET).
- **Critical for:** Heat-driven droughts in Maharashtra, Sahel, and Central USA.

## II. Crop-Specific Drought Thresholds (FAO Standards)
### 1. Wheat (Triticum aestivum)
- **Yield Response Factor (Ky):** 1.15 (Sensitive to water stress).
- **Critical Stages:** Booting, Heading, and Flowering.
- **Advice:** If Rainfall < 100mm in the last 60 days of the growing season, immediate supplemental irrigation is required.

### 2. Maize (Zea mays)
- **Yield Response Factor (Ky):** 1.25 (Highly sensitive).
- **Critical Stages:** Silking and Tasseling.
- **Threshold:** Soil moisture depletion > 50% leads to irreversible ear size reduction.

## III. Regional Soil Water Retention Profiles
### 1. Vertisols (Black Cotton Soils) - Common in India/Sudan
- **Characteristics:** High clay content, expands when wet, cracks when dry.
- **Water Handling:** High water-holding capacity but difficult to manage; requires slow irrigation to prevent runoff via cracks.

### 2. Arenosols (Sandy Soils) - Common in Sahel/Coastal regions
- **Characteristics:** High permeability, low nutrient retention.
- **Water Handling:** Dries out rapidly. If rainfall stops for > 7 days, "Critical" risk should be assigned regardless of total rainfall volume.

---

## IV. Global Drought Mitigation Best Practices
### 1. Conservation Agriculture (CA)
- **Principles:** Minimum soil disturbance, permanent soil cover (mulch), and crop rotation.
- **Benefit:** Increases water infiltration by 30-50%.

### 2. Farmer-Managed Natural Regeneration (FMNR)
- **Application:** African drylands.
- **Benefit:** Systematic regrowth of trees provides shade, lowering local ground temperatures and soil evaporation.
